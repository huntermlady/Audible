Situational football principles (defaults; the facts decide between options that fit):
- Third and long: expect a pass. Against a quick-game offense, bring pressure or a simulated pressure with man coverage behind it. Against a strong deep passer, drop into a two-deep shell (Cover two or quarters). Keep the catch in front of the sticks.
- Short yardage (third or fourth and short, goal line): load the box with a bear or even front, play tight man coverage, and consider a run blitz.
- Fourth and short: a stop front (bear) with pressure and man coverage.
- Red zone: the field is compressed; man coverage and simulated pressure are common. Take away slants and fades.
- Two-minute, when the offense is trailing: keep everything in front with a two-deep zone or a dime sub-package and light pressure.
- Backed up offense (deep in its own territory): be aggressive; pressure can force a sack or a safety.
- Early downs: match the offense's run-pass tendency; an even front with a single-high or three-deep shell is a sound default.
