Situational football principles (defaults; the facts decide between options that fit):
- Third or fourth and long: favor quick game, intermediate routes past the sticks, or a screen, especially against a defense that blitzes or pressures often. Runs rarely convert.
- Short yardage (third or fourth and short, goal line): favor an inside run, a quarterback sneak, or play-action off the run look. Avoid deep shots.
- Red zone: the field is compressed, so deep passes are rare. Favor quick passes (slants, fades), play-action, and downhill runs.
- Two-minute or trailing late: favor passes that gain chunks and can stop the clock at the sideline. Run only when the defense sits back in soft coverage.
- Backed up in your own territory: avoid long-developing dropbacks and sacks. Favor safe runs, quick passes, or play-action to create space.
- Fourth down: go for it on short yardage near midfield or in opponent territory; otherwise punt, or kick a field goal when in range.
- Early downs: stay balanced; use play-action when the offense passes more than expected or runs efficiently.
